Read research_v5/BRIEF.txt and research_v5/devharness.py first and obey every rule in the brief. Your directory is research_v5/color/ ; create files only there.

Goal: measure whether label-free COLOR evidence improves the existing matchers. The existing HGB and CNN models are grayscale-only. Known caveat from an earlier audit: some query crops are achromatic while their positive region is also achromatic but nearby negatives are colored; other queries contain colored hatch ink; query crops can also pick up colored ink from overlapping text or boundaries, so a hard veto can destroy recall.

Experiments, all scored with devharness.report on all 55 development queries, gating the cached scores devharness.cached_scores(devharness.HGB_CACHE) and devharness.cached_scores(devharness.DOMAIN_CACHE) and their AND (min) fusion:
1. Per-pixel neighborhood chroma features computed from the full RGB drawing (not from the reviewed coordinates): local saturation/chroma and hue histogram of ink pixels in windows of 16 and 32 px around each pixel (integral images), sampled at the reviewed coordinates.
2. Query color signature from the exact query crop: fraction of chromatic ink and hue histogram of ink pixels (use robust statistics so that a small amount of colored text does not dominate).
3. Soft gates: score * g where g compares local and query color signatures (achromatic-vs-chromatic mismatch and hue distance), with a small family (at most about 12 global settings) of gate strengths. Report every setting, not only the best, and state that the best is development-selected.
4. Report per-document how many queries are helped or hurt by the best gate (use devharness.summarize_decisions per subset of queries at a fixed threshold), so we can see whether a gain is broad or carried by one sheet.

Save all JSON in research_v5/color/ and keep research_v5/color/findings.txt with exact settings and harness numbers. Run .venv/bin/ruff check research_v5/color at the end. Spend at most about 90 minutes. Finish with a plain-text summary of what helped, what hurt, and numbers.
