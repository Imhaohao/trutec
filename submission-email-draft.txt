Ryan,

I built a system that finds the regions of a construction drawing that match a supplied hatch example and writes a binary mask at the original drawing dimensions. It first cleans the query crop: text glyphs and isolated lines are removed, while families of parallel or connected strokes are kept as the pattern. A texture classifier then scores each location against the cleaned query. A second classifier decides each 8-pixel cell from the evidence in windows of 16 to 256 pixels around it, so text and dimension lines printed on top of a hatched area stay with that area. It also compares line orientation, line spacing and mark size with the query, and it was trained with extra weight on look-alike patterns from the same sheet. A fixed ink-color gate lowers scores where the surrounding ink color disagrees with the query.

The primary result is the frozen v14 pipeline, evaluated by the unchanged official evaluator on all 57 native public-validation masks across 13 documents:

- Document-macro IoU: 90.7746%
- Pooled recall: 97.4179%
- Pooled precision: 74.1873%
- False-discovery fraction FP/(TP+FP): 25.8127%
- Reviewed-negative FPR FP/(FP+TN): 5.6145%
- TP: 623,112; FP: 216,806; FN: 16,516; TN: 3,644,738

For context, my earlier pipelines scored 75.9317% IoU / 97.5775% recall / 32.1327% precision (v2) and 88.0003% / 98.3112% / 68.6909% (v7). v14 has 24% fewer false-positive pixels than v7. On 11 of the 13 documents, v14 reaches 96.7% precision, 99.0% recall and a 1.4% reviewed-negative FPR. Most remaining errors come from two dense sheets (61.0% precision, 96.0% recall), where several similar patterns sit side by side.

How v14 differs from v7: both classifiers are refit on all labelled training documents (the fit split plus the real development documents), and the second classifier is larger (600 boosting rounds of up to 63 leaves instead of 200 of up to 31). The larger size was chosen by document-grouped cross-validation over the training documents with a rule fixed before running, and v14 was recorded as the primary candidate before it was trained or scored.

On validation exposure: public validation was scored for v1-v3 and v6-v14 (twelve versions), and earlier errors there informed which directions I worked on. These numbers are therefore validation-informed rather than an untouched holdout. No v14 setting was chosen on public validation: the query cleaner, threshold 0.25 and color gate are copied from v7, and the second-stage size was chosen on training data. The pipeline was frozen and hashed before its single validation run. Private-test and whole-sheet accuracy remain unknown. Two public examples are positive-only, and there are no eligible blank pixels for a reviewed-blank FP rate.

Integrity: the entry point infer_v14.py produced masks pixel-identical to the frozen pipeline on a development drawing, and all 75 frozen source and model hashes were verified on the inference machine before the validation run. All 57 validation masks are native-size binary PNGs whose IDs match the requests.

Inference took 1,270.79 seconds for all 57 queries on an Apple M1 Pro with 16 GiB unified memory. Peak memory footprint was 7,588,234,496 bytes, with zero swaps. Runtime covers the whole Python inference process; scoring and packaging are excluded. v14 uses CPU tree models only, with no neural network.
